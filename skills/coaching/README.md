# Coaching

**A one page prep sheet for the manager, so the conversation changes one behavior instead of describing a call.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A list of six things to improve after a call. It is fair and accurate, and nobody can act on it.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Diagnose before choosing an approach
2. Pick one behavior
3. Write the opening self-assessment question
4. Quote the moment
5. Write two rehearsal prompts
6. Set the next observation

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "coach my CSM"
- "feedback on this call"
- "call review"
- "my CSM keeps making the same mistake"
- "how do I give feedback on"

## Who this is for

Written for team leads and heads of customer success. Included in: `cs-team-lead-toolkit`, `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. What you saw: the call or artifact, and the moment that bothered you
2. Whether the person has done this well before, here or elsewhere
3. Their book size and what they are measured on

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- One behavior, never six
- Diagnose first
- Self-assessment before feedback
- Describe the moment, never the person
- Rehearse, do not advise
- Coach the cause upstream
- No next observation means it did not happen
- Keep it out of forecast and portfolio meetings and away from pay talk
