# Advocacy Ask

**You walks away with a call (ask now, wait, or do not ask), the person, one small bounded ask, and what goes back the other way.**

**What goes wrong without it:** Asking for a reference at renewal. The timing turns a favor into a trade, and the customer learns that agreeing has a price.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Run the four disqualifiers
2. Find the moment
3. Pick the person who benefited, not the signer
4. Choose the smallest rung that does the job
5. Surface the approval path before drafting
6. Name what they get
7. Write the register line

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "reference call"
- "case study"
- "testimonial"
- "sales wants a reference"
- "can we use them as a logo"
- "who can we ask for a review"
- "G2 review"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Account, renewal date, and anything open or broken for them
2. What was asked for, by whom (often sales), and for which prospect
3. What the customer has said worked, in their words

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Never ask at renewal
- An open problem outranks a good moment
- The signer writes vendor-management prose
- Small and bounded gets a yes
- Approval kills case studies late
- Consent is per use
- One register across all teams
- A no is data
