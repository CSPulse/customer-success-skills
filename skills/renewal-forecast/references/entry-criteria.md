# Entry criteria and default ranges

Load at step 3. Every percentage below is a common default, set your own. Replace with your landed numbers after two periods.

## Categories

| Category | Entry test (someone else could apply it) | Worst / expected / best |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Commit | Executed order, written confirmation from the decider, or auto-renew with the notice date passed | Signed value in all three. Known contraction from signed amounts counts |
| Likely | Decider named and spoken to this period, no live risk signal, value agreed by the decider (not only a sponsor) | Churn 5% / 0 / 0 of ARR. Contraction 8% / 4% / 0 |
| At risk | A named mechanism with a chain from what was seen to non-renewal | Per group: two outcomes (resolves, does not), not a percentage |
| Unknown | No test above passed, or inside 90 days with no documented risk status | No basis: churn 100% / 25 to 50% / 0. Better: price each account by name |
| Lost | Notice served or decision communicated | Full churn in all three |

## Sums

- Retained = starting ARR - churn - contraction. Churn is whole accounts lost. Contraction is dollars lost from accounts that stay.
- Expected for a bet = (odds it fails x failed outcome) + (odds it resolves x resolved outcome). State the odds and tag them `inferred`.
- Low and high add each bet's worst and best case, so they are wider than a likely outcome. Say so; treat low as a floor, not a plan.
- Expansion: signed in all cases, verbal added at expected, pending added at high. Tag each as `said` or `inferred`.

## Slip versus loss

Ask of every Likely and At risk account: if it renews, could the paper land after period end? Typical causes are board dates, security re-reviews and signature chains. Count it in expected, list it under "Slip, not loss", and note it hurts the period, not annual retention.

## Calibration

Once two periods exist, compare each forecaster's Likely and At risk to what landed. Two tells: At risk that renews almost in full with no play attached (sandbagging), and Likely resting on one sponsor's word (happy ears). Apply an adjustment and show it in the output.
