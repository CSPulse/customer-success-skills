# Escalation

**The first message to send within the hour, written from one line, then the pieces that keep the relationship intact once the incident is fixed.**

**What goes wrong without it:** Going quiet until you have the answer. Silence reads as chaos or concealment, and both are worse than an update with nothing new in it.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Type it
2. Draft the holding statement
3. Draft the forwardable answer
4. Draft the internal ask
5. Set severity and cadence,
6. Repair plan, second pass,

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "their VP is involved"
- "the account is on fire"
- "angry customer escalation"
- "they are threatening to churn"
- "they want a call today"
- "this has gone to their exec"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. What happened and what it cost them, in their terms
2. Who is escalating, and who they answer to
3. What you can commit alone

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Silence is the failure
- Severity sets the cadence
- The contact usually needs cover, not just a fix
- One owner, one version of the facts
- Hold the executive card
- No theory of cause
- A relationship escalation treated as a failure fix returns
- Repeat escalations get an executive sponsor,
- Quiet after resolution is not relief
