# Renewal Risk

**A one-page read you can act on or defend in a forecast call. Built on one idea: usage describes you, the renewal is decided by the buyer.**

**What goes wrong without it:** "At risk, low usage." It names a category, states no mechanism and implies no action, which makes it the most common line in a risk review and the least useful.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Set their clock, not yours
2. Name the decision and the decider
3. Read the signals by weight
4. Tag every piece of evidence
5. State the mechanism as a chain
6. Argue against the read
7. Name one play

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "will they renew"
- "is this account at risk"
- "renewal risk"
- "churn risk"
- "should I be worried about [account]"
- "what's the read on [account]"
- "they asked for our utilization numbers or the notice date"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs and team leads. Included in: `csm-toolkit`, `cs-team-lead-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Account, ARR, renewal date, and the notice or opt-out date if the contract has one
2. Who signs, and when you last spoke to them
3. The one thing that is worrying you (or "nothing, just checking")

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- No decider, no green
- Quiet is not healthy
- Requests for data are negotiation prep
- A champion's optimism is evidence about the champion
- Most at-risk accounts renew smaller, not never
- Fix the window honestly
- Correlated risks are one bet
- Do not invent a mechanism
