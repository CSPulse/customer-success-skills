# Signals, by decision weight

Work down the tiers. A tier 1 signal outranks any amount of tier 3 comfort. Read `context-adjustments.md` before calling a deviation, because the same observation means different things by segment, pricing model, motion and industry.

## Tier 1: decision mechanism

- Economic buyer or executive sponsor changed, or is about to
- The user cannot name who signs, or has never met them
- Single-threaded: one relationship, no recovery path if that person leaves
- No agreed, currently true value metric. Nobody can state the number the customer bought
- Sponsor-specific silence while the working team stays engaged

## Tier 2: commercial and procurement

The buyer starts before the vendor does. The highest-value signals here are routinely misread as helpfulness.

- Requests for utilization data, seat counts, contract copies, invoice history, or the notice date
- A mid-term security, legal or vendor-risk re-review of an existing vendor (a re-buy in disguise, or routine governance; find out which)
- A request to shorten the term, move to monthly, or remove auto-renew (they are buying optionality)
- A new procurement, vendor-management or FinOps party in the thread
- Competitor or benchmark pricing mentioned in conversation
- A downgrade request is lagging, not leading

## Tier 3: usage shape, never usage level

Level is close to worthless. Shape, direction and composition carry the signal.

- Trajectory over snapshot. A drop inside the healthy band still matters
- Breadth against depth. One power user carrying the account is one resignation from zero
- Admin against end-user activity. Heavy config, permission audits or bulk export with flat end-user activity often means migration prep or an audit
- Failure rate as well as usage rate. "It does not work for them" and "they do not use it" need opposite plays
- Integrations disconnecting is disentangling
- Zero new users provisioned in 90 days is a stall even at flat aggregate usage

## Tier 4: support and sentiment

Ticket volume alone signals nothing. Rising means friction, falling from a previously active account means resignation. What predicts:

- Type: bulk export or API extraction requests, deprovisioning questions, permission audits, and above all questions about cancellation terms or notice periods
- Who is filing: the champion stopped and only junior admins remain
- Language shift toward "unacceptable" and "disappointed"
- Non-response. B2B survey response is low enough that silence is the default and cannot be read as consent. Who used to respond and stopped is the signal

## Tier 5: organizational

Low frequency, high impact: their acquisition or merger, layoffs, a new functional leader, a reorg, a cost-out program, a vendor consolidation initiative. A new executive reviews everything the predecessor bought. When they are acquired it is usually a standardization risk on the acquirer's timeline. When they acquire it is an expansion chance plus a bake-off.
