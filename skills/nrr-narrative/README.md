# NRR Narrative

**A one-page retention story a board can follow and a CFO cannot poke a hole in, built by taking the number apart before explaining it.**

**What goes wrong without it:** Presenting the retention number without its parts. It survives one question from anyone who has seen this before and loses the room after that.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Decompose before narrating
2. Compute the pair
3. Say the basis
4. Find the concentration
5. Separate what recurs
6. Name what the number hides,
7. Write three sentences first

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "NRR"
- "net revenue retention"
- "why did NRR drop"
- "retention slide for the board"
- "explain our retention numbers"
- "what is our NRR hiding"

## Who this is for

Written for heads of customer success. Included in: `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. The period, starting ARR from existing customers, and any of: expansion, price uplift, contraction, churn.
2. Cohort cuts if they exist, the three largest expansions, and where churn sat (segment, channel, cohort).
3. The audience, and the decision the number feeds.

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- GRR before NRR, always together
- Price is not expansion
- Contraction gets its own number
- Blended flatters a fast-growing base
- Deferred churn is not avoided churn
- A changed definition goes first
- Disclose concentration yourself
- Show dollars beside percents
- Report lagging and leading together
