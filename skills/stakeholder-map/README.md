# Stakeholder Map

**An org chart says what people are called. This says what each person can do to the account, who you has really met, and what to do about the gaps.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A complete contact list for an account where nobody can name the decider.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Sort by position, not title
2. Add the met column
3. Say who decides first
4. Count the threads
5. Track movement
6. Give each gap a route and a date

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "stakeholder map"
- "who actually decides"
- "who signs this"
- "map the account"
- "we are single-threaded"
- "who do I need to know"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs and team leads. Included in: `csm-toolkit`, `cs-team-lead-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. The people you knows on the account, with role and last contact date
2. Who you thinks signs, and whether they have ever spoken to them
3. Anything that changed lately: new executive, someone gone quiet, someone left

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Every account has a sceptic
- Seniority is not influence
- A group call is not a relationship
- A champion's optimism is evidence about the champion
- Sponsor silence while the working team stays engaged
- A gap without a route is only a description
- The map goes stale
