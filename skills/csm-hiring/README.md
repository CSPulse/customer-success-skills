# CSM Hiring

**An interview kit a hiring manager can run next week: the same questions for every candidate, a work sample that shows judgement, and a way to decide that does not depend on who liked whom.**

**What goes wrong without it:** Hiring on rapport alone, then finding out in month three that the new CSM cannot read an account or write a clear update.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Write the scorecard from the outcomes
2. Choose eight questions
3. Assign who asks what
4. Build the work sample
5. Write the rubric
6. Write the debrief

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "interview kit for a CSM"
- "hiring a CSM"
- "CSM interview questions"
- "work sample for a CSM candidate"
- "scorecard for the CSM role"
- "how do I tell a good CSM from a nice one"

## Who this is for

Written for team leads and heads of customer success. Included in: `cs-team-lead-toolkit`, `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Segment, rough book (accounts and ARR), and whether the hire owns renewals and expansion
2. Level and the experience you expects
3. What the last strong hire and the last weak one did differently

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Judgement and ownership outrank charm
- Ask every candidate the same job-related questions
- The work sample is the best signal
- Score before you discuss
- Ask what went wrong, then who did it
- Listen for dollars
- Probe the "we"
- Anchor scores to evidence, not to the person
