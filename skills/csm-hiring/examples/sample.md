Input the user gave: "Hiring a mid-market CSM, 35 accounts, about $2.7M, owns renewals but not expansion. 2 to 5 years of experience. Our last great hire could say no to a customer; the last bad one was everyone's friend and missed two notice dates."*

```
CSM INTERVIEW KIT: Customer Success Manager | mid-market | 2 to 5 years
Year-one outcomes: own ~$2.7M of renewals with a written read on each by T-120 days;
lead 4 QBRs a quarter that end in a decision; no account loses a notice date unseen;
recaps out within one working day.

Scorecard (weights are common defaults, set your own)
| Competency | Why it matters here | Weight |
| Prioritization under load | 35 accounts, one person | 20 |
| Commercial judgement | owns renewals and concessions | 20 |
| Ownership | the bad hire never said what went wrong | 15 |
| Hard customer | monthly, not rare | 10 |
| Executive communication | sponsors change | 10 |
| Written clarity | most of the job is email | 10 |
| Data comfort | reads usage and the forecast | 10 |
| Technical fluency | product is configurable, not simple | 5 |
Hiring bar: no 1 on prioritization, commercial or ownership; weighted total of at least 70 of 100.
Weighted total = sum of (score / 4 x weight). Example: all 3s = 75.

Questions (same order for every candidate)
1. Prioritization: You inherit 40 accounts on Monday, six emails marked urgent and a renewal next week you did not know about. Walk me through your first four hours.
   Strong: checks that renewal's notice date and signer first, ranks by consequence and date, says what they will drop and who they tell.
   Weak: answers in inbox order, "I would work late". Probe: what did you not do?
2. Commercial: Tell me about a renewal where the customer wanted a discount or fewer seats.
   Strong: states the dollars, set a walk-away beforehand, traded term or scope. Weak: gave price to keep the relationship, cannot say the figure. Probe: what did you get back?
3. Ownership: Tell me about an account you lost or nearly lost.
   Strong: names the date they could have seen it and their own miss. Weak: the customer, a reorg or the product. Probe: when did you first know?
4. Hard customer: Describe a customer who was angry with you or your company. What happened in 24 hours?
   Strong: acknowledged, named owner, set the next update time, wrote it down. Weak: defended, or escalated without owning. Probe: what did you say first?
5. Executive: You have 10 minutes with a CFO who has never heard of us. What do you say first?
   Strong: opens with their number and one ask. Weak: product tour. Probe: what if they cut you off at two minutes?
6. Written: Tell me about bad news you gave in writing. What was the first sentence?
   Strong: the news, the date and the ask. Weak: context first, news in paragraph three. Probe: show me how you would write it.
7. Data: A health score says green and the customer has gone quiet. Do you believe it?
   Strong: asks what it measures, checks the signer and recent changes, compares to the account's own trend. Weak: trusts it, or dismisses all data. Probe: what would you pull first?
8. Technical: Tell me about the hardest product you supported. A customer asks something you cannot answer. Now what?
   Strong: "I will confirm by Thursday", brings an engineer, never guesses. Weak: guesses, hides it. Probe: how did you learn the product?

Who asks what: Round 1 (hiring manager, 30 min): Q1 to Q3. Round 2 (panel of senior CSM
and support lead, 45 min): Q4 to Q8, both score independently. Round 3 (manager and senior
CSM): work sample, 30 min to write, 15 to discuss.

Work sample: Brightwater Clinics, $96k, renews Mon 1 Mar 2027, 60 day notice. Packet:
- Account notes say "usage healthy, 36 of 60 seats active"; the admin export shows 22 active in the last 30 days (36 is anyone who logged in within 90).
- Champion Rhea (operations manager) replied twice: "all good". Signer is the CFO, never met.
- Rhea asked last week for the order form and seat use by site.
- Three open tickets on data import, oldest 34 days. Onboarding goal: intake time 14 to 9 minutes; the last QBR slide says 12, with no customer baseline.
Traps: the opt-out date is Thu 31 Dec 2026, not 1 Mar; the seat figure has two measures; the CFO is unmet.
Rubric, 0 to 2 per line (16 max): names signer and says unmet / computes the 31 Dec opt-out / gives a dollar range, not a color / tags said, seen, inferred / catches the seat measures / gives a mechanism or says unknown / one play with owner, date and failing checkpoint / lists what they could not check.
Pass: 11 of 16, with the opt-out date and signer lines both at 2.

Red flags: takes credit for team results and cannot say their part; cannot name a failure;
blames customers; talks only about relationships and never dollars; guesses product answers.

Debrief (each interviewer fills it before the meeting)
- Candidate, interviewer, date. Score 1 to 4 per competency, with the line they said.
- Work sample score out of 16; which traps caught.
- Red flags seen, quoted.
- Discussion: any competency where scores differ by more than 1 point, evidence first.
- Decision: hire / no hire / need more evidence, in one reason. Risks to coach if hired.

What I could not check
- Whether 70 of 100 is the right bar for your market; test it on your two best current CSMs
- Local rules on question content and unpaid work samples; HR or legal should confirm
- Product specifics for Q8, which should be adapted to your product
```
