# By scenario

The method in SKILL.md applies to all four. This file covers what changes. Notice periods, credit sizes and timelines below are a common default: set your own.

## 1. Commercial change (price rise, term change, lost discount)

- **Your side is not at fault, so do not apologize for the decision.** It tells the customer your company erred and invites a request to undo it.
- **Value case first, in their measure.** What their business got, not your release notes. Without it the number is argued alone and the user loses.
- **Check the number against what they can benchmark:** their other renewals, public pricing, a competitor quote. A number that fails one goes to procurement.
- **Respect their budget cycle.** The same rise delivered before planning is often accepted and fought after.
- **Expect "we will look at alternatives."** Decide beforehand if it is real. Acknowledge it, do not flinch, do not discount at once.
- **A shorter term, monthly billing or no auto-renew is optionality, not savings.** It usually costs more than the discount.
- **Do not give:** a permanent carve-out to a structural change. Offer a one-year phase-in instead.

## 2. Roadmap no

- **The cost is the ambiguity.** A clear no is survivable. A maybe lives in their business case for two years.
- **Use words that cannot be heard as "not yet":** "We are not going to build this." If it is under review, give the date they will know by.
- **Explain in terms of their problem, not your backlog.** Offer the other route: workaround, partner, services, or the honest "this product will not do this".
- **Ask what the request was for.** It arrives pre-translated into a solution, and the underlying job is the part worth taking to product.
- **If they built a business case on it,** it is a commercial conversation. Find what was promised, by whom and where, first.
- **Do not give:** a date you do not control. It turns a roadmap no into a missed commitment in three months.

## 3. Missed commitment

- **It is a trust event, not a schedule event.** Their real question is what else is not true.
- **Tell them before the date.** A slip announced early is project management. The same slip announced late is a credibility problem.
- **Call it a miss,** not "an updated timeline".
- **Re-commit once, to a date the user controls.** If the new date depends on someone who already missed, give the next update date instead.
- **One sentence on what changes so it does not repeat,** only if true.
- **Ask what it cost them.** It separates an inconvenience from a broken plan with their executive attached.
- **Do not give:** an unasked credit to close the discomfort. It prices every future commitment.

## 4. Costly failure (outage, bug, data or billing error)

- **Facts, timeline, impact, nothing else until the cause is known.**
- **Own the impact now, the cause later:** "this stopped your team for six hours and that is on us" needs no root cause.
- **Give the immediate action and the prevention separately,** so the follow-up does not read as correcting the first answer.
- **No credit, refund or liability wording without commercial authority.** Say you are asking, with a date.
- **If there is a number, get commercial and legal involved before anything is written.**
- **Update on a fixed cadence even with no news.** Silence reads as chaos or concealment.
- **Do not give:** a root cause on the call, a promise it cannot recur, or an uncleared number.

## Common to all four

Say the thing, take the impact, explain only if asked, then move to the remedy. Reversing that order makes every scenario worse.
