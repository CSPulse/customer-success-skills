# Book Review

**The monthly or quarterly look at the whole book, written so it ends in three decisions rather than a report.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A report on the whole book that ends without a decision. A few accounts or one renewal month carry the risk, and nobody sees it until it becomes a cliff.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Total the book and put changes first
2. Measure concentration
3. Lay out the renewal calendar
4. Weight health by ARR
5. Find coverage gaps
6. Estimate expansion
7. End with three decisions

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "review my whole book"
- "quarterly book review"
- "concentration risk in my book"
- "renewal cliff"
- "my book on a page"
- "where is my book exposed"

## Who this is for

Written for team leads and heads of customer success. Included in: `cs-team-lead-toolkit`, `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. The list: account, ARR, renewal date, notice period, health read (green, yellow, red) and last real touch
2. The date and headline numbers of the last review, if one exists
3. Your touch cadence and whether the decider has been met on each renewing account

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Count dollars, not accounts
- Look for the cliff before the average
- Quiet and not green together is the finding
- Every start needs a stop
- Keep the shape identical every time
- Standing attention is for accounts triage would starve
- Expansion is an estimate until the customer names a need
- If the cadence cannot be met in a calm month, the book is too big
