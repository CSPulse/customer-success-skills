# CSQL Motion

**A design for how CSMs surface expansion signals and hand them to sellers without spending the trust that produced them.**

**What goes wrong without it:** The lead dropped into a queue. A CSM passes a real expansion signal, someone with no context works it, the customer gets a cold sales approach, and the CSM never passes another one.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Choose the position
2. Write two or three qualifying signals,
3. Design the handoff as an introduction
4. Protect the customer
5. Settle attribution before the first lead moves
6. Pick two measures
7. Write kill criteria and a review date,

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "CSQL"
- "CS sourced pipeline"
- "should CS sell"
- "hand expansion leads to sales"
- "CS to sales handoff for expansion"
- "how do we credit CS for expansion"

## Who this is for

Written for heads of customer success. Included in: `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Who works expansion today (AE, account manager, expansion team) and what CS owns commercially.
2. Three to five recent expansions that happened, and what was visible beforehand.
3. How CSMs and sellers are paid on expansion and retention.

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- One bad handoff ends the flow of signals
- Qualify on behavior or a stated need, never satisfaction
- The CSM stays in the room
- No cold outreach to a warm account
- A "no" must be free
- Attribution disputes start after the first large deal
- Measure relationship cost, not just conversion
- A program with no end condition is never ended
