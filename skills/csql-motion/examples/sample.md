```
CSQL MOTION: Hollowmere Systems | 12 CSMs | 4 sellers working expansion

Call: CS spots and hands off. No seller contacts a CSM's customer until they have read
the context note. If the AEs will not commit to that, do not launch.

Position: spot and hand off. CSMs are bonused on gross retention and have no selling
training, so asking them to run deals would give leadership neither.

Signals (CSM names which fired and quotes the evidence)
1. Stated need with a named owner, including an explicit ask ("can we do this for
   the other division?").
2. Usage boundary: seats active at 90% or more of licensed for 30 days, or volume at
   85% of commit (a common default, set your own).
3. Organizational trigger: new team, region, merger or mandate extends the use case.
"They seem happy" and a high health score do not qualify.

Handoff: the CSM sends the note, then emails one introduction to the seller and the
customer, and stays on the first call until the need is confirmed. The seller
acknowledges within 1 business day and reaches the customer within 5 (a common
default, set your own). No finance or CFO outreach unless the sponsor asks for it.

Context note, filled for one example (Arbor Logistics)
1. Signal: usage boundary and stated need. 118 of 125 seats active for 30 days (seen,
   admin console). Ops director Inez asked on 9 Feb to add the Dallas hub, about 40
   users (said, call 9 Feb).
2. Said so far: she asked what 40 seats would cost. I quoted nothing.
3. Avoid: price before the Dallas use case is confirmed. December support week went
   badly and she raised it twice.
4. Skeptic: a finance analyst who pushed back on last year's renewal.
5. Ask of the seller: confirm the use case, then size it. I join the first call.

Customer protection: value reviews and sales calls are separate meetings. The CSM
raises a possibility and never negotiates. A declined expansion changes nothing about
service, and a CSM's GRR bonus is never reduced by a lost expansion.

Attribution, agreed before the first lead moves
- Sourced: the CSM named a signal and filed the note before any seller contact on that
  opportunity. Influenced: the seller already had an open opportunity.
- Both teams see a sourced deal on their dashboards. Revenue counts once.
- CSM gets a $500 spiff per closed-won sourced deal and a line in their review
  (assumed, confirm with finance). Sales ops owns the number.

Measures: CSQL-to-won conversion against the sellers' inbound, same quarter.
Relationship cost: risk flags, engagement and satisfaction of passed accounts against
matched accounts not passed, monthly. Spread: CSMs who passed one or more signals.

Kill criteria, reviewed 30 Jul 2027 (two quarters after launch)
- CSQL win rate below the sellers' inbound win rate
- Passed accounts show more risk flags than matched accounts not passed
- Fewer than 6 of 12 CSMs contributed
- Any customer escalation caused by a handoff: pause that week, review within 7 days

What I could not check
- Whether the AEs will accept the read-first rule: this is the precondition
- The spiff amount and whether finance will fund it
- Historical expansion data to test the three signals against past wins
```
