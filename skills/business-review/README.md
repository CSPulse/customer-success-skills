# Business Review

**A slide-by-slide story for a review that ends in a decision, or a clear call that this account should get a written report instead.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A deck of usage charts for a customer who wanted to know whether the product is worth keeping. The meeting replays dashboards and decides nothing.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Run the gate first
2. Write the ask and the belief
3. Pick 3 or 4 headline numbers in their outcome language
4. Write the slides as full-sentence headlines
5. Shape for the room
6. Pre-brief the champion
7. Draft the 24 hour follow-up

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "QBR"
- "EBR"
- "should I run a QBR"
- "prep the business review"
- "build the review deck"
- "what should I present to [account]"
- "exec review next week"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs and team leads. Included in: `csm-toolkit`, `cs-team-lead-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Account, review date, and who is invited (is the person who signs coming)
2. The goal the customer stated at purchase or last review
3. Anything open or sore since the last review

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Do not run a review when
- Send an async report to accounts where a meeting costs more than it returns
- The ask goes first
- Activity is not value
- When value cannot be measured
- Never surprise the champion in front of their boss
- Do not disguise an expansion pitch as a review
- Data without context gets skipped
