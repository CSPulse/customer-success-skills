# Mutual Action Plan

**A plan the customer can read, edit and forward: their goal in their words, dated rows owned by both sides, and the decisions that gate them.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A shared plan where only the vendor owns tasks. It is a to-do list the customer never signed up for, and it gives no warning when their side slips.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Write the goal in their words
2. Work backward from the fixed date
3. Give both sides rows
4. Make decisions rows
5. List needs, then risks
6. Set the cadence
7. Cut to twelve rows or fewer

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "mutual action plan"
- "shared plan with the customer"
- "joint plan with dates"
- "customer-facing timeline"
- "owners on both sides"
- "they keep missing their dates"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. The type and the outcome in the customer's own words, with the date that matters to them
2. Named people on their side and the sponsor, and your own team
3. Dates already fixed: contract, freezes, holidays, budget or board dates

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Dates are agreed, not imposed
- Both sides own rows
- Twelve rows or fewer (a common default, set your own)
- Use their words for things
- Decisions are rows with a date
- A slipping row on their side is an early warning
- Write nothing you would not show them
- Do not invent their commitments
