# Coverage Model

**A tier model you can defend to leadership: who gets a named CSM, who is pooled, who is digital, what that costs in hours, and what the team is choosing not to see.**

**What goes wrong without it:** Starting from headcount. Divide the accounts by the CSMs, call the result a ratio and describe it as a strategy.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Segment on three axes
2. Estimate cost to serve
3. Compute the ratio as an output
4. Define each tier in five lines
5. Close the gap by choice
6. Design the transitions
7. Back-test against churn

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "coverage model"
- "CSM ratio"
- "how many accounts per CSM"
- "do we need more headcount"
- "high touch low touch"
- "we cannot cover the long tail"

## Who this is for

Written for heads of customer success. Included in: `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. The book: account count and ARR by size band, or the list with ARR and complexity notes (integrations, business units, regulated).
2. The team: CSM headcount, and any pooled, support or digital capacity.
3. The last two years of churn: account, ARR, segment, stated reason.

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- The ratio is an output
- Tier on complexity as well as revenue
- A cadence you cannot meet is a promise to be broken quietly
- Write down what each tier loses
- Call it unserved unless digital has four parts
- The downward move is the dangerous one
- Temporary elevation needs an end date
- A model that fails the back-test is only plausible
