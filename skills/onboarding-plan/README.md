# Onboarding Plan

**A plan the customer can point to later: what value means for them, who owns each step, and what would tell you in week three that the year is going badly.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A finished onboarding checklist attached to a customer who cannot say what changed.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Define value per customer before kickoff
2. Audit the handover for what is missing
3. Validate, do not re-interrogate
4. Separate first value from full value
5. Name customer owners with hours per week
6. Write the watchlist
7. Confirm the criteria in writing the same day

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "onboarding plan"
- "just closed a new customer"
- "kickoff is next week"
- "first 90 days"
- "time to first value"
- "is this customer really live"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. What was sold, the signature date, and the contract value
2. Who signed, who will use it, and the problem the customer said they were solving
3. What sales promised that may not be in the contract

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Done means the customer can walk you through a result
- A target without a baseline cannot be falsified
- Diagnose a refusal to commit
- Fallback ladder when no outcome metric exists
- Unwritten promises become scope disputes in month three
- Sceptics belong on the stakeholder list
- Plan the security and IT review
- Perceived momentum outranks green metrics
