# Offboarding

**A clean exit: their data out early, an honest account of why, and a relationship that can come back.**

**What goes wrong without it:** The account that goes cold the moment it is marked lost. The data export is slow, the last conversation never happens, and a solvable loss becomes a customer who tells people how it ended.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Confirm it is over
2. Do the contractual work first and fast
3. Ask for the exit conversation separately
4. Keep reason and mechanism apart
5. Plan the open door
6. Hand over internally once
7. Draft the last message

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "offboarding"
- "they gave notice"
- "they have decided to leave"
- "wind down the account"
- "exit conversation"
- "data export request"
- "how do I handle a clean exit"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Account, contract end date, and when and how notice was served
2. Who decided, and what they said the reason is
3. Anything the contract says about data return, deletion, and a final invoice

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Stop selling
- Make the data export faster than required
- No surprise final invoice
- Hold the exit conversation apart from the wind-down
- Do not defend, correct or badmouth the replacement
- The reason given is rarely the mechanism
- A departing champion is a future buyer
- The honest internal record is the only one that changes anything
