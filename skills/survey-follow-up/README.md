# Survey Follow Up

**A survey response turned into the right move, a draft, and an entry for the account record. Draft only, you sends.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A survey score that sits in a spreadsheet. The detractor hears nothing back, and the promoter gets asked for a reference too often.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Classify the response
2. Read who is speaking
3. Set it against the account
4. Pick the move and its deadline
5. Draft the first message
6. Write the log entry
7. Check anonymity

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "NPS response"
- "detractor"
- "low CSAT"
- "the survey came back"
- "they scored us a"
- "we got a promoter"
- "nobody answered the survey"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Score and scale (NPS 0 to 10, CSAT 1 to 5), the exact comment, and the date
2. Who answered: name, role, buyer or end user. Was the survey anonymous?
3. How many were sent and how many replied, plus the account's renewal date and any open issue

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Call a detractor, do not email
- Listen first and do not defend
- Do not argue with a score
- Never average across people
- Silence is not consent
- Close the loop in writing, including a no
- Ask a promoter for advocacy only if the account is healthy, has proof, and has not been asked in about 90 days (a common default, set your own)
- Log person-level risk as person-level
