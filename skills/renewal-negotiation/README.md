# Renewal Negotiation

**A one-page plan to walk into a price conversation knowing what you will trade, what you will not, and where you walk away.**

**What goes wrong without it:** Negotiating price before value is agreed. With nothing on the other side of the scale, you argue about a number and lose.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Gate it
2. Pull the contract facts
3. Write both walk-aways
4. Name what each party is measured on
5. Build the ladder
6. Write the not-on-the-table sentence
7. Map the time

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "they want a discount"
- "procurement is involved"
- "how much can I give"
- "they want to cut seats"
- "competitor quoted us half"
- "prep the renewal negotiation"
- "they are asking for utilization data"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Account, ARR, term end date, notice period, and whether silence renews or lapses
2. What they asked for (discount, fewer seats, competitor price) and from whom
3. The lowest number your company would accept

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Price needs value on the other side of the scale
- Nothing is given free
- Prefer a one-time credit to a rate cut
- Separate rightsizing from rate
- Do not match a quote you have not seen
- Utilization data and contract copies are the negotiation starting
- The opt-out date is leverage for whoever knows it
- Never close a number on the last three days of a quarter
