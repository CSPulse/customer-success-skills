# By update type

Load the section that matches. The method in SKILL.md applies to all six.

## Incident or outage
- Speed beats completeness. First message: impact, scope, owner, next update time. No cause yet.
- Hold the cadence even with nothing new: "No change, next update 16:00" is worth sending. Silence reads as chaos.
- Scope precisely (region, product area, time window) so the unaffected can stop reading.
- The post-incident note is a separate document, sent once the cause is established, reviewed by someone with commercial authority.

## Delay to something promised
- Announce before the date, not after they planned around it.
- Call it a slip. Re-commit once, to a date you control. If the new date depends on a team that already missed, promise the next update date instead.
- Most delays matter to a handful. That is a segmentation problem, not a wording problem.

## Deprecation or end of life
- Give the longest notice you can. First screen holds three things: the stop date, what replaces it, the migration cost in effort and money.
- Heavy users get a person and a plan; everyone else gets one line.
- Decide the extension or migration help you can offer before you send, so replies are consistent.
- Never pair it with an unrelated announcement.

## Pricing or packaging
- Check contractual notice (days and method) first. Named accounts get a conversation before the notice.
- Give the reason once and hold the same words. Say what does not change.

## Planned maintenance
- Subject carries date, window and duration. Body: what is unavailable, what keeps working, any action.
- State the timezone every time. Repeat close to the date. Send a one-line "it is over" message.

## Change of CSM
- Introduce a person with one specific relevant detail. List what carries over: open items, commitments, history.
- Overlap with a joint call. Near a renewal or mid-escalation, delay the change if you can; otherwise the outgoing CSM hands over live.
- Never let them learn it from an auto-reply or a bounce.
