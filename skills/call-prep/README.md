# Call Prep

**A prep sheet you can read in two minutes: what the call must produce, what will derail it, and what to ask for.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A call that goes well, produces a recap and moves nothing. Everyone leaves satisfied, and the next call has the same agenda.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Write the 10-minute version first, always
2. Make the outcome failable
3. Work the room
4. Find what changed on their side
5. Name the landmine
6. Write three questions
7. Set the ask, the fallback ask, the time budget and the cut line

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "prep me for my call with"
- "call prep"
- "I have a call in an hour"
- "what should I ask them"
- "talking points for"
- "walking into this call cold"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. Who is on the call (names, roles) and what you last heard from them
2. What you wants out of it
3. Length of the call and time left to prepare

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- No ask, no call
- "Align", "touch base" and "walk them through" are not outcomes
- Name the landmine
- Prepare questions, not a deck
- Budget for about 70 percent of the clock
- Close with a yes or no, not "let me know"
- A recurring call with no outcome is a call they are looking for a reason to decline
- Never invent attendee wants
