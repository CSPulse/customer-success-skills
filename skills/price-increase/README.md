# Price Increase

**Design the uplift as a program: who gets it, who is protected, when notice must go, what you will trade, and how you will know what it cost.**

**What goes wrong without it:** A blanket increase sent to every account. It lands hardest on the accounts with a fragile sponsor or unproven value, and those are the ones that leave.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Fix the policy
2. Write the protect list first
3. Build the timeline from the contract
4. Set the concession ladder before the first call
5. Draft the one message
6. Set monitoring

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "price increase"
- "renewal uplift"
- "raise prices on my book"
- "which accounts do we protect"
- "notice period for the increase"
- "price rise letter"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs, team leads and heads of customer success. Included in: `csm-toolkit`, `cs-team-lead-toolkit`, `cs-leader-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. The uplift (percent or dollars), the renewals it applies to, and the reason in customer terms
2. The book: account, ARR, renewal date, price-change notice period, any price cap or lock
3. Per account, what you know about proof of value, the sponsor and any competitor or save plan

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- Notice comes from the contract, not from habit
- Notice, value, then a call. Never an email alone
- Segment before you send
- Trade, do not discount
- One owner, one message
- Count the cost on its own line
- An increase with no new value is a churn event
- Hold, do not force, where value is unproved
