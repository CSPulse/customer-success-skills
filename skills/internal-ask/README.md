# Internal Ask

**You walks away with an internal request shaped for the people who must act, an honest size and severity, and the message to close the loop with the customer.**

**What goes wrong without it:** "Can someone please look at this." It names no decision, no deadline and no owner, so it sinks to the bottom of a queue. Or a customer email forwarded into a channel with the information stripped out.

Part of [customer-success-skills](../../#readme).

## What it does

1. Pick the lane
2. Lane A: translate to the job
3. Lane A: size it honestly
4. Lane B: write one sentence
5. Check severity against your company's own definition
6. Plan the loop

## What you get

The answer comes first, then the reasoning. Every output ends with a list of what the skill could not check, so a gap shows up as a gap.

See a filled example for an invented company in [examples/sample.md](examples/sample.md).

Say things like:

- "feature request"
- "raise this with product"
- "I need engineering to look at this"
- "get leadership involved"
- "I need an exception"
- "nobody is picking this up"
- "how do I get this prioritized"

## Who this is for

Written for CSMs. Included in: `csm-toolkit`.

## What it needs

Nothing is required. Paste what you have and it works with that. These inputs help most:

1. What the customer or account needs, and the date it matters
2. Who in your company owns the answer, if known
3. What you have already tried

Anything you leave out is listed under "What I could not check" instead of blocking the skill. No connector, file or other skill is needed.

## Rules it follows

- A request needs a name, an action and a date
- Forwarding the customer email transfers the request but none of the information
- Count jobs, not feature names
- Never inflate
- Use the existing path
- Do not go over anyone silently
- Close the loop, especially on a no
- Escalation is not delegation
